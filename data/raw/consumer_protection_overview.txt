Consumer Rights Overview (India)

Every consumer in India has a set of basic rights: the right to be protected
against goods and services that are hazardous to life and property; the
right to be informed about the quality, quantity, and price of goods; the
right to choose from a variety of goods at competitive prices; the right to
be heard and to have their interests considered; the right to seek redress
against unfair trade practices; and the right to consumer education.

If a seller refuses to replace, repair, or refund a defective product, a
consumer can first raise a written complaint with the seller or
manufacturer. If this does not resolve the issue, the consumer can approach
the Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission at the district, state, or
national level, depending on the value of the goods or services involved.
Complaints can also be filed through the National Consumer Helpline
(1915 / consumerhelpline.gov.in), which mediates between consumers and
businesses before escalation.

Typical documents needed to file a complaint: proof of purchase (bill or
invoice), any written communication with the seller, photos of the
defective product if applicable, and a copy of the complaint letter sent to
the seller.

Consumer commissions generally aim to dispose of complaints within a few
months, though actual timelines vary based on case complexity and the
backlog at each commission.

Responsible authority: District/State/National Consumer Disputes Redressal
Commission, or the National Consumer Helpline for informal mediation first.
