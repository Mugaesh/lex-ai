Basic Labour Rights — Overview

Workers in India are entitled to certain baseline protections regardless of
the specific job: timely payment of wages, a written appointment letter or
terms of employment where applicable, safe working conditions, and
protection from unlawful termination without notice or due process in many
categories of employment. Minimum wages are fixed by state governments and
vary by category of work and skill level.

If an employer withholds wages, terminates employment without following
due process, or denies statutory benefits (such as Provident Fund or ESI
where applicable), a worker can first raise the issue in writing with the
employer/HR. If unresolved, a complaint can be filed with the local Labour
Commissioner's office, which has the authority to summon the employer and
mediate or refer the matter for adjudication before a Labour Court.

For workplace harassment, most establishments with 10 or more employees are
required to have an Internal Complaints Committee (ICC) under the sexual
harassment prevention law; if the establishment doesn't have one, a
complaint can go to the district-level Local Complaints Committee.

Documents that help: appointment letter or employment contract, salary
slips, any written communication about the dispute, and attendance records
if available.

Responsible authority: Office of the Labour Commissioner (state or
central, depending on the establishment); Internal/Local Complaints
Committee for harassment matters.
