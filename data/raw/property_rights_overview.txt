Property Rights and Disputes — Overview

Buying, inheriting, or holding property in India involves several official
records: the sale deed (registered at the local Sub-Registrar's office),
the encumbrance certificate (proof the property is free of loans/legal
dues), mutation records (updating ownership in revenue records), and
property tax receipts. A property transaction is only legally complete once
the sale deed is registered; an unregistered agreement does not transfer
ownership.

For disputes over illegal possession or encroachment, a property owner can
first send a legal notice to the occupant, then file a civil suit for
possession or an injunction in the local civil court. In urgent cases of
forcible dispossession, a police complaint can also be filed alongside the
civil remedy.

For disputes with a real estate developer (delayed possession, false
advertising, deviation from approved plans), a complaint can be filed with
the state Real Estate Regulatory Authority (RERA), which was set up
specifically to protect home buyers and can order refunds, compensation, or
completion of the project.

Inheritance disputes (who is entitled to ancestral or family property) are
generally governed by the personal law applicable to the family (Hindu
Succession Act, Muslim personal law, Indian Succession Act, etc.) and are
resolved through a civil court or, where uncontested, through a succession
certificate from the relevant court.

Documents that help: sale deed, encumbrance certificate, property tax
receipts, mutation extract, and any RERA registration number for the
project (for builder disputes).

Responsible authority: Sub-Registrar's office (registration), State RERA
Authority (builder/developer disputes), local Civil Court (possession and
inheritance disputes).
