Right to Information (RTI) — Overview

The Right to Information Act, 2005 allows any citizen of India to request
information from a "public authority" — essentially any government body or
body substantially funded by the government. This can be used to check the
status of an application (such as a ration card, pension, or certificate),
understand why a decision was made, or get copies of official records.

To file an RTI request, a citizen writes an application addressed to the
Public Information Officer (PIO) of the relevant department, clearly
stating the information sought. Applications can usually be submitted in
person, by post, or online through the department's or the central RTI
portal, along with a small application fee (fee waived for applicants
below the poverty line, subject to proof).

The PIO is required to respond within 30 days of receiving the request; if
the information concerns the life or liberty of a person, the response
must come within 48 hours. If no response is received, or the applicant is
unsatisfied with the response, they can file a first appeal with the
department's Appellate Authority, and subsequently a second appeal with the
State or Central Information Commission.

Documents needed: a clearly written application specifying the exact
information sought, proof of fee payment, and (if applicable) proof of BPL
status for a fee waiver.

Responsible authority: Public Information Officer (PIO) of the relevant
department; Appellate Authority for first appeal; State/Central Information
Commission for second appeal.
