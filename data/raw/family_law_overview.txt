Family Law and Domestic Protection — Overview

Marriage in India can be registered under a personal law (such as the Hindu
Marriage Act) or the Special Marriage Act for inter-religious or civil
marriages. A registered marriage certificate is often required for
practical purposes such as passport applications, visas, or claiming
spousal benefits.

A person facing domestic violence — physical, emotional, verbal, sexual, or
economic abuse from a spouse or family member — can seek protection under
the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005. Relief available
includes a protection order (restraining the abuser), a residence order
(the right to stay in the shared household), monetary relief, and custody
orders for children. A complaint can be filed with the local Protection
Officer, the police, or directly before a Magistrate; free assistance is
also available through the nearest One Stop Centre or the Women Helpline
(181).

For separation, a spouse can pursue mutual-consent divorce (faster, both
parties agree) or contested divorce (filed before the Family Court on
grounds such as cruelty, desertion, or others recognized under the
applicable personal law). Maintenance (financial support) can be claimed by
a spouse or children during or after divorce proceedings under the relevant
personal law or Section 125 of the Code of Criminal Procedure.

Documents that help: marriage certificate, proof of residence, any evidence
of abuse (medical reports, messages, photographs), and income proof of both
parties (for maintenance claims).

Responsible authority: Protection Officer / local police (domestic
violence complaints), Family Court (divorce, custody, maintenance), Women
Helpline 181 or the nearest One Stop Centre for immediate support.
