Reporting Cyber Crime and Online Fraud — Overview

Citizens who experience online fraud (UPI/payment fraud, phishing, fake job
offers, digital arrest scams, identity theft, hacking, or online
harassment) can report it through the National Cyber Crime Reporting
Portal at cybercrime.gov.in, or by calling the National Cyber Crime
Helpline number 1930. Financial fraud cases reported quickly (ideally
within a few hours) have a significantly higher chance of the transaction
being reversed or the funds frozen before they are withdrawn.

When reporting, it helps to have: the transaction ID or UTR number (for
payment fraud), screenshots of the fraudulent message or call, the phone
number or account details of the fraudster if known, and a timeline of
what happened.

For non-financial cybercrime (harassment, hacking, fake profiles), a
complaint can also be filed at the nearest police station's Cyber Crime
Cell, in addition to the online portal.

"Digital arrest" scams — where a caller impersonates a police officer,
customs official, or CBI official and claims the victim is under
investigation and must stay on a video call and transfer money — are not a
real legal procedure. No Indian law enforcement agency conducts arrests or
investigations over a phone or video call, and citizens should treat any
such call as a scam and report it immediately via 1930 or
cybercrime.gov.in.

Responsible authority: National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal
(cybercrime.gov.in), National Cyber Crime Helpline (1930), or the local
Cyber Crime Cell at a police station.
