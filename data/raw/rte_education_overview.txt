Right to Education (RTE) — Overview

The Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009 entitles
every child between 6 and 14 years of age to free and compulsory education
in a neighbourhood school. Private unaided schools are required to reserve
at least 25% of entry-level seats for children from economically weaker
sections and disadvantaged groups, who are admitted free of cost with the
government reimbursing the school.

If a school refuses admission to an eligible child, demands a capitation
fee, conducts a screening test/interview for admission (which is
prohibited at the entry level under RTE), or fails to admit a child under
the 25% quota, a parent can file a written complaint with the Block/District
Education Officer, or approach the State Commission for Protection of
Child Rights (SCPCR).

Schools are also prohibited from expelling or detaining a child until the
completion of elementary education (up to Class 8), and from subjecting
children to physical punishment or mental harassment.

Documents that help: proof of age and residence (for neighbourhood school
admission), income/category certificate (for the 25% quota), and any
written communication with the school regarding the refusal or issue.

Responsible authority: Block Education Officer / District Education
Officer; State Commission for Protection of Child Rights (SCPCR) for
escalation.
