Filing a First Information Report (FIR) — Overview

An FIR is the written record a police station prepares when it receives
information about the commission of a cognizable offence (a serious offence
where police can arrest without a warrant, such as theft, assault, or
fraud). Any person who is aware of the commission of such an offence,
including the victim or a witness, can report it.

A citizen can walk into the police station having jurisdiction over the
area where the offence occurred and give the information orally or in
writing. The police officer is required to record it, read it back to the
complainant, and have it signed. The complainant is entitled to receive a
free copy of the FIR once it is registered. Many states also allow FIRs to
be filed online for certain categories of offences through their state
police e-portals.

If the police refuse to register an FIR for a cognizable offence, the
complainant can send the complaint in writing by post to the
Superintendent of Police of the district, who is required to either
investigate it personally or direct that it be investigated. A complainant
can also approach the local Magistrate to direct the police to register
the FIR.

Documents that help when filing: any evidence available (photos, messages,
medical reports if relevant to an injury), identification proof, and a
clear written account of what happened, including date, time, and place.

Responsible authority: The Station House Officer (SHO) of the local police
station; if refused, the Superintendent of Police or the jurisdictional
Magistrate.
