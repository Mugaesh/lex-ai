Senior Citizens' Rights — Overview

Under the Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007,
parents and senior citizens (aged 60 and above) who are unable to maintain
themselves from their own income or property have the legal right to claim
maintenance (food, clothing, residence, and medical care) from their
children or, in the case of childless senior citizens, from relatives who
would inherit their property.

A senior citizen can file an application for maintenance before the local
Maintenance Tribunal, set up specifically under this Act in every district.
The Tribunal can order the children/relatives to pay a monthly maintenance
allowance and can act quickly, without requiring a lawyer, since the Act is
designed to be an accessible, simplified process for elderly applicants.

The Act also allows a senior citizen who has transferred their property to
a child or relative (by gift or otherwise) on the condition of being taken
care of, to have that transfer declared void by the Tribunal if the
condition is not fulfilled — an important protection against property
being taken and the senior citizen then being neglected or abandoned.

For elder abuse, neglect, or abandonment, a complaint can be made to the
police, the Maintenance Tribunal, or through the Elder Helpline (14567),
which operates in most states and can coordinate emergency assistance,
counselling, and legal aid.

Documents that help: age proof, income proof (to show inability to
self-maintain), and any documentation of property transferred to the
respondent (for property-related claims).

Responsible authority: District Maintenance Tribunal (constituted under
the Act), local police (abuse/safety concerns), Elder Helpline 14567.
